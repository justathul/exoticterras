How to build a bioactive terrarium

A bioactive terrarium is a small living ecosystem. Plants grow in the soil, and a cleanup crew of springtails and isopods eats mould, dead leaves and waste, so the tank stays clean with very little work from you. Here is the build we recommend for a first terrarium.

1. Pick the right container

Any clear glass or acrylic tank works. Closed tanks hold humidity and suit ferns, moss and fittonia. Open tanks and bowls dry out faster and suit hardier plants. Whatever you choose, keep the glass out of direct sun, because it heats up quickly.

2. Drainage layer

Put 2–3 cm of clay pebbles (LECA) or small stones at the bottom. This holds spare water below the soil so roots don’t sit in a puddle.

3. Mesh barrier

Cut a piece of fibreglass or plastic mesh to fit and lay it over the drainage layer. It keeps the soil from sinking into the water below.

4. Substrate

Use 4–6 cm of a soil mix that drains well, for example coco coir with some bark and a little sand. Avoid ordinary garden soil with added fertiliser. Slightly damp, never soggy, is the goal.

5. Plants and hardscape

Place taller plants at the back and low ones in front. Moss, fittonia and small ferns do well in humid tanks. Add a piece of driftwood or bark, because it gives the plants structure and the cleanup crew somewhere to hide.

6. Leaf litter

Cover the soil with a layer of dry leaves. This is the food supply for isopods and springtails, and it keeps the soil surface moist.

7. Add the cleanup crew

Add springtails first, then isopods a few days later. For a small to medium tank, a starter culture of springtails and 5–10 isopods is plenty. Easy species such as Powder Orange or Dwarf White are good first choices. Our Bioactive Cleanup Pack is put together for this.

8. Water and ongoing care

Mist lightly when the surface looks dry. If mould appears, open the lid for a day and add more springtails. Drop in a few leaves or a small piece of vegetable every few weeks if the crew looks hungry.

Common mistakes

  • Too much water, which causes mould and root rot.
  • Direct sunlight on a closed tank.
  • Adding fertiliser, which can harm the cleanup crew.
  • Skipping the drainage layer.
