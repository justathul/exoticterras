Springtails: why you need them and how to keep them

Springtails are tiny, soil-living insect relatives that jump when disturbed. In a terrarium or vivarium they eat mould, fungus and decaying matter, which makes them one of the most useful animals you can add.

What springtails do for your tank

  • Eat mould before it spreads over soil and wood.
  • Break down dead leaves and waste into nutrients for plants.
  • Live in the soil and leaf litter, and they never harm plants.
  • Breed quickly, so a small group grows into a steady population.

How many should I add?

For a small tank, one starter culture is enough. For a large or very humid setup, add more, or add a second batch after a couple of weeks. They need moisture to multiply, so they do best in damp, humid enclosures.

Adding them to your tank

Tip the culture onto the leaf litter or soil and give the tank a light mist. Don’t worry if you can’t see them at first. They hide in the substrate and come out as the tank settles.

Keeping a spare culture

  1. Use a small lidded box with a few tiny air holes.
  2. Fill it with 3–4 cm of damp coco coir or a charcoal and coir mix.
  3. Add a few drops of yeast or a pinch of fish food every week or two, and remove anything that goes mouldy.
  4. Keep it moist but not wet, out of direct sun.

Springtails and isopods together

They complement each other. Springtails handle mould and fungus; isopods deal with larger leaf litter and wood. Using both gives the most complete cleanup crew for bioactive terrariums and reptile or dart frog vivariums.
